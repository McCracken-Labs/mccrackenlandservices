Real property, descent and distribution at a glance · Texas Estates Code Chapter 201

Who inherits Texas real property when a person dies without a will. Read each row as the whole interest in that property, split among the heirs shown. Community property and separate property follow different rules, so characterize each asset first. One date runs through the community property rules: September 1, 1993, when the share passing to children changed. Citations are to the Texas Estates Code, Chapter 201.

Full statute text: Texas Estates Code, Chapter 201. This chart shows real property. Separate personal property divides differently: with children the surviving spouse takes one-third and the children two-thirds (§ 201.002(b)); community personal property follows the same community rules shown above.

This chart covers Texas real property and is prepared for landmen and title professionals as a working field tool. It is not legal advice and does not substitute for the advice of qualified Texas oil and gas counsel. Distributions turn on the community or separate character of each asset and on facts such as prior marriages and predeceased heirs. A predeceased heir's share passes to that heir's descendants by representation. Complex or disputed heirship should be referred to a licensed Texas attorney. Citations are to the Texas Estates Code, effective January 1, 2014, which recodified the former Texas Probate Code.
